Receipt Rights

The store told me to take it up with the manufacturer. Do I have to?

No. Under the Australian Consumer Law, your claim is against the business that sold you the item (s 259). The store can't pass you on to the manufacturer.

Why it's the store's job

  • The consumer guarantees, like goods being of acceptable quality and lasting a reasonable time, apply to the sale. Section 259 gives you the right to act against the supplier, which is the business you paid.
  • The store isn't left out of pocket. If the fault is a quality problem, the manufacturer has to reimburse the store for what it costs them to deal with you (s 274). "It's the manufacturer's problem" is something for the store to sort out with the manufacturer, not you.
  • A store policy that says otherwise ("we only handle returns in the first 30 days", "faults go to the manufacturer") can't override the guarantees (s 64).

When going to the manufacturer makes sense

You're allowed to, and sometimes it's easier: if the store has closed or gone out of business, if the manufacturer's warranty service is quick, or if you bought it somewhere awkward to get back to. The law gives you some rights against manufacturers too (s 271). But that's your choice, not something the store can insist on.

Other things stores say

  • "You need the original box." You don't (s 259(7)).
  • "We can only give you a store credit." For a major failure you're entitled to a refund if you choose one, and store credit doesn't count (s 263(5)).
  • "Bring it in." If it's a fridge, a built-in oven or anything else you can't return without significant cost, the store has to collect it at its own expense when you reject it (s 263(2)(b) and (3)).
  • "No receipt, no refund." You need proof you bought it there, but a bank statement or order email will do. More on that

What to say at the counter

"I understand the manufacturer's warranty has ended, but I'm asking under the consumer guarantees in the Australian Consumer Law. Under section 259 my claim is with you as the seller. Can you give me your answer in writing, please?"

Asking for the answer in writing matters. Staff often say no at the counter, and a written refusal is what your state consumer agency or a tribunal will want to see.

If they still won't help

Send a short written claim (email is fine) with a 14-day deadline. If that doesn't work, contact your state or territory consumer agency, such as NSW Fair Trading, Consumer Affairs Victoria or the Queensland Office of Fair Trading. The full list is here.
